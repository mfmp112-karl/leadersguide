Leader's Guide

Learning to Walk in Salvation — A Four-Week Journey Through Romans
For Facilitators, Pastors & Group Leaders
Before You Begin

The Three-Book Framework

While participants study Romans as their core text, this leader's guide draws on two companion books — John and Colossians — to give you a richer, three-dimensional picture to teach from each week.

Book Role in the Study What It Adds
Romans The spine — core text for participants The gospel explained: what salvation is and how it works
John Companion voice — for the leader The Saviour encountered: who Jesus is in personal, relational terms
Colossians Companion voice — for the leader The new self understood: what Christ's lordship means for everyday identity

Romans answers what and how. John answers who — in a way new believers can feel, not just understand. Colossians answers therefore what — grounding identity before behaviour.

📖 Companion Readings — John and Colossians passages that echo the week's Romans theme, with notes on how to use them naturally in discussion
✝️ Who Is Jesus? — A short section you can read aloud or paraphrase to open or close the session, keeping Christ personally visible throughout the theological content
The participants experience a clean, focused Romans study. You carry the fuller picture and bring it in naturally through conversation.
Purpose

Purpose of This Leader's Guide

This guide is written for facilitators, pastors, and group leaders guiding mostly new believers through Romans. Your role is not to lecture deeply or resolve every theological question, but to:

  • Keep the big story in view
  • Help participants follow Paul's flow of thought
  • Create a safe space for questions
  • Anchor understanding of salvation in Scripture
  • Encourage growth and service without pressure
Tone to maintain: clear, calm, grounded, welcoming, non-performative.

"Romans is one long explanation — not separate lessons." Keep reminding participants of this.
Week One
The Big Story & the Human Problem
Creation, Fall, Promise & Romans 1–3

Help participants see salvation within the entire Bible story, not as a religious rule-set.

Suggested Sunday Session Flow (60–75 minutes)

1
Welcome & Orientation 5 min

Explain how the course works. Emphasise questions are welcome.

2
The Bible's Big Story 15 min

Creation → Fall → Promise → Jesus. Keep this narrative, not preachy.

3
Romans 1–3 Discussion 25 min

Humanity's shared problem. Sin as broken relationship and worship.

4
Wrap-Up & Next Reading 10 min

Reinforce why grace is necessary. Introduce Romans 4–5.

Key Teaching Anchors

  • Sin is deeper than behavior
  • Everyone stands equally in need
  • The Law reveals the problem but cannot fix it
Leader Reminder: Avoid naming specific sins; keep focus on condition, not actions.
📖 Companion Readings — Week 1
These passages run alongside Romans 1–3. You do not need to assign them to participants — use them to enrich your teaching and discussion.
John 3:19–20

Personalises the problem. Paul says humanity is under sin; John shows why — people preferred darkness to light. This helps new believers see that sin is not just rule-breaking but a rejection of God at the heart level.

Colossians 1:21

Captures the relational cost of sin. Paul in Colossians says we were alienated from God and hostile in mind. Use this to show that the problem Romans describes is not abstract — it broke a relationship.

✝️ Who Is Jesus? — Week 1
Key Passage: John 1:1–14

Before Romans explains the problem, John shows us who entered into it. Jesus is not a solution that arrived out of nowhere — He is the eternal Word, the one through whom all things were made, who stepped into the very brokenness Romans describes. The world did not receive Him, yet He came anyway. This is the Saviour behind the gospel Paul is explaining. When participants feel the weight of Romans 1–3, remind them: the one who exposes our condition is also the one who came to rescue us from it.

Common Questions You May Hear

"

Are good people still sinners?

"

Why would God judge everyone?

Response Strategy: Point back to Paul's argument, not personal opinion.
Week Two
God's Solution: Justification by Faith
Romans 4–5

Help participants clearly understand what salvation is and what it is not.

Suggested Sunday Session Flow

1
Review Week 1 5 min

Restate the problem briefly.

2
Romans 4–5 Discussion 30 min

Abraham and faith. Righteousness as status.

3
Peace With God 15 min

Emphasise assurance, not emotion.

4
Wrap-Up & Next Reading 10 min

Introduce Romans 6–8.

Key Teaching Anchors

  • Faith is trust, not effort
  • Grace precedes obedience
  • Peace with God is objective, not circumstantial
Leader Reminder: Gently correct performance-based thinking.
📖 Companion Readings — Week 2
These passages run alongside Romans 4–5. Use them to deepen and personalise the session.
John 3:16–18

Makes justification intimate. Romans explains the mechanics of righteousness by faith; John gives it a heartbeat — God loved, God gave, we believe. This is particularly powerful for new believers who may understand the doctrine but not yet feel its warmth.

Colossians 2:13–14

Provides one of the most vivid images of justification in all of Scripture. The record of debt against us — every charge, every failure — was nailed to the cross and cancelled. Use this to make the concept of righteousness as a status feel concrete and final.

✝️ Who Is Jesus? — Week 2
Key Passage: John 14:6

Romans 4–5 explains that salvation comes through faith — but faith in what, exactly? John 14:6 makes it clear: not a system, not a religion, not a set of rules, but a person. Jesus does not point the way — He is the way. He does not teach the truth — He is the truth. He does not offer life as a programme — He is the life. When participants grasp this, justification by faith stops being an abstract doctrine and becomes a personal response to a living Saviour.

Common Questions You May Hear

"

Does this mean works don't matter?

"

What if my faith feels weak?

Response Strategy: Emphasize direction of trust, not strength of feeling.
Week Three
New Life, New Power
Romans 6–8

Help participants understand how change happens without guilt or fear.

Suggested Sunday Session Flow

1
Review Justification 5 min

Re-anchor identity before behavior.

2
Romans 6–8 Discussion 35 min

Union with Christ. Role of the Spirit.

3
Struggle & Growth 15 min

Normalize ongoing struggle.

4
Wrap-Up & Next Reading 10 min

Introduce Romans 12–16.

Key Teaching Anchors

  • Identity leads behavior
  • No condemnation in Christ
  • Growth is progressive
Leader Reminder: Avoid implying instant victory over sin.
📖 Companion Readings — Week 3
These passages run alongside Romans 6–8. Use them to give organic, lived texture to the theological truths Paul is unpacking.
John 15:1–8

The vine and branches passage gives the best picture of how transformation actually works. Romans 6–8 explains union with Christ theologically; John 15 shows it organically. Change does not come from trying harder — it comes from staying connected to the source. This is a deeply encouraging image for new believers who feel the gap between who they are and who they want to be.

Colossians 3:1–4

Anchors identity before behaviour. You died. Your life is hidden with Christ in God. This is the Colossians parallel to Romans 6 — same truth, different angle. Use it to reinforce that new life is not about striving upward but about living from what is already true.

✝️ Who Is Jesus? — Week 3
Key Passage: John 16:7, 13

Romans 8 introduces the Holy Spirit as the power behind new life — but new believers often wonder: who exactly is the Spirit, and why did He come? John 16 answers directly. Jesus told His disciples it was better that He go, because He would send the Spirit in His place. The Spirit is not a force or a feeling — He is the continuing presence of Jesus, leading believers into truth, convicting, guiding, and empowering. Romans 8's promise of no condemnation and life in the Spirit is personal because the Spirit Himself is personal.

Common Questions You May Hear

"

Why do I still struggle if I'm saved?

"

How do I know the Spirit is working?

Response Strategy: Point to Romans 8 assurance, not self-analysis.
Week Four
Living Out Salvation Together
Romans 12–16

Help participants see obedience and service as a response to grace, not a requirement for acceptance.

Suggested Sunday Session Flow

1
Course Recap 10 min

Walk through Romans 1–8 briefly.

2
Romans 12–16 Discussion 30 min

Renewed minds. Love and humility.

3
Serving & Belonging 15 min

Introduce church involvement naturally.

4
Closing Encouragement 10 min

Emphasise ongoing growth.

Key Teaching Anchors

  • Transformation flows from gratitude
  • Gifts are for serving others
  • Community is essential for growth
Leader Reminder: Invite, do not pressure, participation.
📖 Companion Readings — Week 4
These passages run alongside Romans 12–16. Use them to ground the call to community in the words and prayer of Jesus Himself.
John 13:34–35

The command that preceded everything Romans 12–16 describes. Before Paul wrote about love, humility, and unity, Jesus gave His disciples one defining instruction: love one another as He loved them. This is not a general ethic — it is a gospel-rooted command. Use it to show participants that community life is not a church programme; it is the mark of a saved people.

Colossians 3:12–17

The most practical clothing list in all of Scripture for how saved people treat one another. Compassion, kindness, humility, patience, forgiveness, peace, and gratitude — all flowing from the identity established in Christ. This is the Colossians complement to Romans 12, and together they give rich material for discussing what love and community actually look like day-to-day.

✝️ Who Is Jesus? — Week 4
Key Passage: John 17:20–23

In John 17, on the night before His crucifixion, Jesus prayed — and He prayed for us. Not just His disciples in the room, but everyone who would believe through their message. He asked the Father that we would be one, just as He and the Father are one. The unity Romans 12–16 calls us to is not a social goal — it is a reflection of the inner life of God Himself, and it was the last great prayer of Jesus before the cross. As you close this final week, remind your group: the community they are being called into was on the heart of Jesus before He went to die for it.

Common Questions You May Hear

"

What should I do next?

"

How do I keep growing?

Response Strategy: Point to community, Scripture, and steady practices.

Final Leader Encouragement

Your success is not measured by perfect understanding, but by:

Clarity of the gospel
Confidence in Scripture
Desire to grow
Willingness to walk together
Romans does the heavy lifting — your role is to guide, not carry.
